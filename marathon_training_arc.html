The Stablemaster's Method · John Starrett

Build the fitness for a year. Spend it in one race.

John Starrett coaches the marathon as a slow climb, not a scramble. A newer runner spends roughly a year layering seven blocks on top of each other — each one earning the right to the next — until the body is ready to race once, hard. Then it recovers, and the fitness settles in higher than it started.

≈ 1 year, first jog → start line  ·  4 runs a week  ·  one marathon a year  ·  scroll the ridge ↓

Putting money in the bank

The seven blocks of the build

Each block has one job and a gate you have to pass before the next one starts. Weeks shown are John's rough guidance for a slower, newer runner — treat them as a floor to repeat, not a countdown. The whole build runs about a year.

0
Pre-Foundation≈ 2 wk

The on-ramp for a true beginner: jog-walk and very short easy runs, just to get the body used to being on its feet. Skippable if you can already run 30+ minutes comfortably.

GateCan run ~35 min easy, continuous.
1
Foundation≈ 7–10 wk

Build easy volume to 3×65 min + one 90 min run. No tempo, no intervals — just time on feet to toughen the musculoskeletal system and learn to run by feel. This is where beginners get hurt if they rush it.

Gate3×65 + 90 min easy, comfortably.
2
Pre-Super Base≈ 7–8 wk

Rewire the stride: turn a hip-flexor "shuffle" into a glute-driven run. Two tools — 8-second hill blasts and aerobic strides — build running economy without real fatigue. The long run stays at 90 min.

GateForm feels automatic; economy improved.
3
Super Base≈ 14 wk

The first real fitness block. A midweek tempo session and a Saturday "endurance spine" long run descend and re-ascend the V-shaped valley of death (8×5→…→20×1 and back), getting faster while the spine holds it together.

GateStrong and durable — then race a benchmark half.
🏅
🏁 Benchmark Halfrace day

A first small "spend." Coming off the Super Base, John says to race a 10K or half marathon to get a benchmark of where you are — before any marathon-specific work. The result tells you what to build toward and calibrates your paces.

ThenUse the time to set up the Bridge.
4
Marathon Bridge≈ 16–20 wk

Transition from "strong" to "sharp." Tighter recoveries and 10K–10-mile–half-marathon sessions get you fast over the half. John calls the underlying idea temporal-flow training — building resistance to fatigue. Effectively makes you half-marathon fit.

Race along the wayDrop in 5K / 10K / half races through the buildup — the Bridge is built to get you fast over the half, so racing it is on-plan.
5
Marathon Specific≈ 8–12 wk

Now it gets marathon-specific. A hard half-marathon 10–12 weeks out sets your marathon pace (≈ 2× half + 6 min). Then long runs move toward marathon pace — 12→14→18 miles at pace — peaking about 4–5 weeks out. John's warning: most people overdo this phase.

GatePeak long run done, then taper.
★
🏁 The Marathon1 day

The summit. After a short taper, you race — once. This is the withdrawal: a year of banked fitness, spent on purpose. Race by effort, not ego.

ThenRecover — the climb resolves higher than it began.

Training is banking

Every easy run, hill blast and endurance spine is a deposit. The blocks compound — you don't skip ahead, because the next block spends the balance the last one built.

"Training is putting money in the bank. Racing is spending it."

Racing is spending

Race for a purpose, not a target. One marathon a year, not two — two marathons is copying elites, who do it for money. Let the 5K/10K/half PBs along the way be the everyday withdrawals.

"Race for a marker, not a target."

Spending it — and what comes after

After the race: recover, then climb from higher up

The marathon isn't the end of the arc — it's a "super session." Handled right, your baseline fitness ends up ahead of where it was on race morning. Handled wrong, you dig a hole.

01 · RECOVER

Let the system reset

Metabolically you feel okay in 7–10 days, but the central nervous system needs a solid three weeks after a hard marathon. Don't rush it. This is non-negotiable for the rebound to happen.

≈ 3 weeks
02 · BRIDGE BACK

Transition in gently

Come back with light, easy running and a few gentle sessions — a mini bridge — before any real building. You're re-opening the account, not making big deposits yet.

≈ 1–3 weeks
03 · CLIMB AGAIN

Start the next arc higher

Re-enter the cycle — usually back around the Super Base or Bridge, not Foundation. Your default fitness is now higher, so the next year's build starts from a better base.

next block

The rebound, in John's words: "Your default fitness will be ahead of where you were when you ran the marathon, because the marathon acts as a super session — as long as you recover properly from it." An optional backup marathon 4–6 weeks later is possible if the first went wrong and the fitness wasn't all spent — but it isn't ideal.

His method

The block sequence is his

Foundation → Pre-Super Base → Super Base → benchmark race → Marathon Bridge → Marathon Specific → marathon → recover is John's stated system, repeated across several episodes and the YouTube interview.

Ep. 044, Ep. 049, Ep. 013; YouTube "Coaching Beginners to Olympians"

His guidance

Race a half after the Super Base — and during the Bridge

"At this point now I'd get them to race, maybe a 10K or a half marathon and get a benchmark of where they are." The Bridge itself "effectively helps you get really fit for half marathons," built on "10K, a tickle, 10-mile, half-marathon" sessions — so racing shorter events through it is on-plan, not a distraction.

Ep. 044 (benchmark race); Ep. 013 (Bridge = half-marathon fitness)

Tension to know

How long is the Super Base?

He calls it "14 weeks" as its canonical length (and your workbook uses that), but in Ep. 013 he also describes it running "about four, four-and-a-half months." Both are his — the shorter figure is the structured block; the longer includes lead-in/variation. Treat 14 wk as the core.

Ep. 044 (14 wk) vs Ep. 013 (~4–4.5 mo)

Bridge lengths & the pace math
